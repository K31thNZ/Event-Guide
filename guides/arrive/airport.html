Moscow airports & arrival day

Which airport you'll land at, what to expect at border control with the new biometric process, and the fastest way into the city.

Updated June 2026 10 min read
Jump to: Four airportsBorder controlInto the cityFirst hours checklist

Moscow is served by four airports, each connected to different airlines and areas of the city. Understanding which one you'll land at — and how to reach the city from it — saves significant stress on arrival day. The good news: all four have fast, affordable rail links into the centre.

Critical: Always check your airport code (SVO, DME, VKO, ZIA) before travelling. Moscow's airports are far from each other and not connected airside. Mistaking one for another can be catastrophic. They are not interchangeable on the same day.

The four Moscow airports

Sheremetyevo (SVO)

Most international flights

Russia's busiest airport and the hub for Aeroflot and SkyTeam partners. Most long-haul international arrivals land here, including flights from Istanbul, Dubai, and Belgrade — the main routes for Western expats arriving via indirect connections. Terminals D, E, F handle international flights; connected by an internal shuttle. Modern facilities, decent English signage throughout.

Aeroexpress to Belorussky station: 35–45 min, every 30 min, ₽700
~30 km north-west of city centre

Domodedovo (DME)

Asia / CIS routes

Moscow's second-largest airport, around 45 km south-east of the city. Historically the hub for oneworld alliance members and now serves a wide range of flights from Asia and CIS countries. Single large terminal with clear signage, reviewed as well-organised with cooperative border control staff. Good English signage and a straightforward layout.

Aeroexpress to Paveletsky station: 40–50 min, every 30 min, ₽420
~45 km south-east of city centre

Vnukovo (VKO)

Domestic / Caucasus

Primary hub for UTair, Pobeda, and Russian domestic flights to the Caucasus and Siberia. Some charter and CIS international routes also use Vnukovo. Less busy than SVO and DME; generally quicker to move through. If you're connecting from a Russian domestic flight, this is where you may land.

Aeroexpress to Kievsky station: 35 min, every 30 min, ₽420
~28 km south-west of city centre

Zhukovsky (ZIA)

Charter / specific routes

A smaller facility around 40 km south-east. Used for specific charter flights and some scheduled services. Check your itinerary carefully if you see ZIA — it has no Aeroexpress link and requires a bus or taxi to reach the city. Less common for arriving expats.

Bus to Kazan station: ~1.5 hrs depending on traffic
~40 km south-east of city centre

What happens at border control in 2026

The process at Moscow airports has changed significantly since 2024. Here is what to expect step by step, based on updated reporting from russiable.com (May 2026).

Step 1: Immigration queue

Follow signs for "Passport Control" / "Пограничный контроль" immediately after disembarking. Queues at peak times can be long — allow 30–60 minutes. Do not use your phone in the immigration queue; officers notice. Have your passport, visa, and any supporting documents ready in hand.

Step 2: Biometric collection (new since December 2024)

At passport control, officers will take a high-resolution facial photograph and your fingerprints as part of Decree 1510, which has been operating as an experiment at all four Moscow airports since December 2024. This is mandatory for all foreign nationals and cannot be declined. Your data is entered into Russia's Unified Biometric System. The process takes 2–3 minutes.

Step 3: Digital migration record (paper card no longer issued at airports)

As of 2026 at Moscow's airports, the paper migration card that was previously issued to all arrivals is no longer used. The record is now fully digital — the officer enters your data into the system. You will receive either a passport stamp at some checkpoints, or nothing physical at others. Your hotel will be able to verify your entry using your passport number. Note: at land border crossings, the paper card is still in use.

Step 4: Customs

After immigration, proceed to the baggage hall and then customs. Most arrivals can use the green channel (nothing to declare). The standard personal allowance is 10,000 euros equivalent in goods. Do not carry euro banknotes in quantities that look commercial — EU law restricts their export to Russia, and Russian customs may question large quantities.

Don't chat up the officers. Multiple sources confirm immigration officers at Moscow airports are professional but not conversational. Answer questions directly and briefly. Have your accommodation address written down in Cyrillic in case they ask where you're staying.

Getting from the airport to the city

The Aeroexpress train is the correct answer for every Moscow airport. It is reliable, punctual, affordable, and avoids Moscow's notorious traffic. At peak hours a taxi can take 2–3 times as long as the train for the same journey.

SVO (Sheremetyevo) Belorussky station 35 min Every 30 min ₽520
DME (Domodedovo) Paveletsky station 45 min Every 30 min ₽520
VKO (Vnukovo) Kievsky station 35 min Every 30 min ₽520

Fares from aeroexpress.ru (January 2026). SVO single fare ₽700; DME and VKO ₽420. Vnukovo frequency is every 60 min, not 30 min. Round-trip tickets available at a discount. Foreign Visa/Mastercard not accepted — pay cash rubles or MIR card.

At each terminal station, connect to the Moscow metro for onward travel into the city. Belorussky connects to the Circle Line and the green Zamoskvoretskaya line; Paveletsky to the Circle Line and the dark-green Zamoskvoretskaya line; Kievsky to the Circle Line and the dark-blue Arbatsko-Pokrovskaya line. All are 1–3 stops from major central hubs.

Pay with cash rubles or a Russian MIR card — the Aeroexpress ticket machines do not accept foreign Visa/Mastercard. Machines have an English-language option. You can also buy tickets at the ticket counter inside the terminal.

The first hours — practical checklist

  • Have cash rubles. Bring enough for your Aeroexpress ticket, a metro card top-up, and first night expenses. Foreign cards do not work anywhere in Russia.
  • Use hotel WiFi, not mobile data. Foreign SIMs experience a 24-hour data block on first connecting to Russian networks (since Oct 2025). Your hotel WiFi works immediately.
  • Take the Aeroexpress, not a taxi. Taxi touts inside terminals charge inflated prices. If you want a taxi, use Yandex Go (app) once you have WiFi.
  • Your hotel registers you. If staying in a hotel, they handle your mandatory 7-day address registration automatically on check-in. Confirm they've done it within 24 hours.
  • Photograph your passport and visa immediately. Keep digital copies in email or cloud storage in case originals are lost.
