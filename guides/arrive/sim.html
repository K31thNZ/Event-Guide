SIM card & internet setup in Moscow

Getting connected is more involved than it used to be — but entirely manageable with preparation. Here's exactly what to do and, crucially, what to do before you board the plane.

Updated June 2026 8 min read
Jump to: Before you fly Getting a SIM Which operator Home internet Blocked services
Prepare before departure: Since October 2025, foreign SIM cards and eSIMs experience a 24-hour mobile data block when first connecting to a Russian network. Download any apps, configure tools, and test your setup before you land — it is significantly harder to do this from inside Russia.

What to do before you fly

This is not optional preparation — it is the most important section on this page. Several things that are easy to do from outside Russia become significantly more difficult once you're inside.

  • Download and test any VPN tools you plan to use. Most major commercial VPN apps are blocked inside Russia. Research and install obfuscated alternatives before departure. Test that they connect successfully from your home country.
  • Download offline maps. Google Maps offline maps for Moscow and surrounding areas. Maps.me is a good alternative. You may not have data on arrival day.
  • Download Yandex apps. Yandex Maps, Yandex Taxi (now just "Taxi"), and Yandex Delivery work excellently in Moscow and are available globally on app stores. Set them up before you arrive.
  • Save important contacts and documents offline. Embassy number, accommodation address in Cyrillic, your flight details, your contact's Russian phone number.
  • Check your phone is unlocked. You'll need to insert a Russian SIM. If your phone is carrier-locked, get it unlocked before departure.
  • Note your Google/Apple account region. Some apps are not available in the Russian store region. Download them while in your home country.

Getting a Russian SIM card

Getting a Russian SIM card as a foreigner is a more involved process than in most countries, following regulatory changes introduced over the past two years. Anonymous SIM purchases are no longer possible — all new SIM registrations require identity verification linked to Russia's Gosuslugi state portal.

What you need

  • Passport with valid Russian visa
  • Migration card from border entry
  • Russian address registration — this is why it's worth registering at your hotel first
  • Biometric verification — either in-store via the operator's system or via a Russian bank's app linked to Gosuslugi

The practical sequence: arrive → register at hotel (they do it for you) → visit a mobile operator shop with your documents → complete biometric verification in-store → receive SIM.

Use your hotel's WiFi for the first 24 hours. Don't panic about not having mobile data immediately. Every hotel has WiFi; use it to get oriented, contact people, and let your registration be processed before heading to the operator shop.

Which mobile operator to choose

MTS (МТС)

Russia's largest mobile operator. Excellent coverage in Moscow, strong 4G/5G network. Good value unlimited data plans. English-language customer service available at flagship stores. Most popular among expats.

Most recommended

Beeline (Билайн)

Second-largest operator. Competitive pricing, reliable coverage across Moscow. Popular unlimited packages. Good digital app for account management.

Good value

Megafon (МегаФон)

Strong network, good data speeds in the city centre. Slightly more expensive than MTS/Beeline but often has strong coverage in outer districts and the Moscow region.

Strong coverage

T2 (Tele2)

Budget-focused operator with competitive pricing. Coverage in central Moscow is good; patchy in some outer areas. Good for cost-conscious expats who primarily use WiFi.

Budget option

Unlimited data plans with all four operators start from around 500–800 rubles per month. Moscow has excellent 4G and growing 5G coverage. You can also get dual-SIM setups — useful if you want to keep a foreign SIM active for travel.

Home internet (broadband)

Home broadband in Moscow is fast, affordable, and straightforward to set up. Average speeds in the city are among the highest in Europe, and prices are very competitive by Western standards.

The main residential broadband providers are Rostelecom (Ростелеком — state-owned, widest reach), Dom.ru (Дом.ру — popular in apartment buildings), Beeline Home (часто bundled with mobile), and MTS Home. Speeds of 100–500 Mbps are standard; 1 Gbps packages are available in most areas. Monthly cost is typically 500–900 rubles.

Your landlord often has an existing broadband contract for the flat. Ask before setting up a new one — many expats simply use the existing connection and split the cost with the landlord.

Blocked services and internet restrictions

Russia's internet filtering system blocks a significant number of services that Western expats use routinely. This is a reality of daily life in Moscow that every newcomer should be prepared for.

✓ Works without restriction
  • Google Search, Gmail, Google Maps
  • Telegram (some throttling)
  • Yandex services (maps, search, taxi, mail)
  • WhatsApp messaging (voice calls blocked)
  • YouTube (very slow — effectively unusable)
  • Most international news sites (some blocked)
✗ Blocked or severely restricted
  • Instagram, Facebook, X/Twitter
  • WhatsApp voice and video calls
  • Signal, Discord, Snapchat, Viber
  • YouTube (throttled to unusable speeds)
  • BBC, many Western news sites
  • Most major commercial VPN apps
Internet restrictions are an evolving situation. The list of blocked services and enforcement methods changes regularly. For current, specific guidance on accessing services you rely on, contact us — we can share what the expat community is currently finding works.
