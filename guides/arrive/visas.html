Visas & registration in Moscow

From choosing the right visa to completing mandatory address registration within 7 days of arrival — the essential legal framework for every expat.

Updated June 2026 12 min read General guidance only — not legal advice
Jump to: Visa types How to apply Migration card 7-day registration Extending your stay
Important: Visa rules and registration requirements can change without notice. This guide reflects the situation as of June 2026. For your specific nationality and circumstances, always check with the Russian embassy or consulate in your country, or contact us for a referral to a specialist.

Visa types for Western nationals

Russia does not currently offer visa-free entry to most Western nationals (the 2019 e-visa programme was suspended in 2022 and a new version was introduced in 2023 but remains limited in scope). Most English-speaking expats from the UK, US, Australia, Canada, and EU countries require a visa to enter Russia for any purpose.

The main visa categories relevant to expats are:

Most common for newcomers

Work visa (трудовая виза)

Issued to foreign nationals with a confirmed job offer from a Russian employer. Requires the employer to obtain a work permit on your behalf. Valid for 1–3 years, renewable. The most stable long-term status for those employed by a Russian entity.

Employer-sponsored
Business & investors

Business visa (деловая виза)

For those with a formal invitation from a Russian business or organisation. Single, double, or multiple-entry options available. Does not permit working for a Russian employer. Commonly used while arranging longer-term status.

No work rights
Students

Student visa (студенческая виза)

Issued with an acceptance letter from a Russian educational institution. Renewable annually. Often used by those studying Russian language intensively. The institution typically assists with registration.

Institution-assisted
Families

Private / Humanitarian visa

For those with close family in Russia or attending specific events. Requires a formal invitation (vyzov) from a Russian citizen or organisation. Less common for first-time arrivals.

Invitation required
Not sure which visa applies to you? Your visa type determines your registration requirements, work rights, and how you extend your stay. Getting this right matters. Contact us and we can point you to a specialist immigration adviser.

How to apply for a Russian visa

Russian visa applications are made through the Russian embassy or consulate in your home country before you travel. Most countries require you to apply in person or via an approved visa processing centre (VFS Global handles Russian visas in many countries).

What you will generally need

  • Valid passport with at least 6 months' validity beyond your intended stay and at least two blank pages
  • Completed visa application form (now submitted online via evisa.kdmid.ru)
  • Passport-size photo meeting Russian specification (35×45mm, white background)
  • Invitation letter (vyzov) from a Russian host — employer, hotel, business partner, or educational institution
  • Travel insurance valid for Russia with minimum €30,000 medical cover
  • Visa fee (varies by nationality, type, and processing speed — typically £50–£150)
Processing time is typically 5–10 working days for standard applications, 1–3 days for express. Apply well in advance — embassy appointment slots can be limited.

The migration card (миграционная карта)

When you enter Russia, you will be given a migration card at the border — a small paper form that is your proof of legal entry. Keep this safe for your entire stay in Russia. You will need it for registration, opening a bank account, renting a flat, and when you leave.

  • You receive half of the migration card when you enter; the other half is taken when you leave
  • Your permitted stay end date is stamped on the card — do not overstay this
  • If you lose it, report immediately to the local MVD (Ministry of Internal Affairs) office
  • Make a photocopy or photograph immediately after arrival
Biometric data collected at entry: Since December 2024, fingerprints and a facial photograph are taken from all foreign nationals at Russian border crossings. This is mandatory and cannot be declined. Your data is held in Russia's Unified Biometric System.

The 7-day registration rule — critical

This is the rule most newcomers underestimate. Under Russian law, all foreign nationals must register their place of residence with the local migration authorities within 7 working days of arriving in Russia. Failure to do so is a finable offence and can complicate visa extensions or future entry.

If you're staying in a hotel or serviced apartment

Hotels and registered serviced apartments handle registration automatically. They will ask for your passport and migration card on check-in and submit the registration paperwork on your behalf. You should receive a registration slip within 24 hours — keep it.

If you're renting a private flat

Your landlord is legally required to register you. In practice, some landlords are reluctant to do so (it creates a paper trail they prefer to avoid). You must either persuade your landlord or find a flat with a landlord willing to register you. Registering at a serviced apartment for the first week while you find a permanent flat is a practical workaround.

Practical tip: Book a hotel or serviced apartment for your first 7–10 days even if you have long-term accommodation arranged. Let the hotel handle registration while you sort out your permanent flat and landlord situation. This is the smoothest approach for most newcomers.

What registration gives you

  • Legal proof of your address in Russia
  • Required for opening a Russian bank account
  • Required for obtaining a Russian SIM card via Gosuslugi
  • Required for enrolling children in school
  • Needed when extending your visa or changing status

Extending your stay and changing status

If you are on a short-term visa and wish to remain in Russia longer, you need to either extend your current visa or change to a different visa category. Extensions must be applied for before your current visa expires — overstaying, even by one day, results in fines and can result in a ban on future entry.

Longer-term residents often progress from a work or business visa to a Temporary Residence Permit (РВП — Razresheniye na Vremennoye Prozhivaniye) after 1–2 years, and eventually a Permanent Residence Permit (ВНЖ — Vid na Zhitelstvo) after 3–5 years. These provide significantly more stability and fewer bureaucratic requirements.
