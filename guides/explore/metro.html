Mastering the Moscow metro

One of the world's great transport systems — and a genuine attraction in its own right. Here's everything you need to use it confidently, with 2026 fares and payment facts.

Updated June 2026 10 min read Source: russiable.com (updated May 2026), Novika transport guide
Jump to: Key facts How to pay Troika card 2026 fares Apps Stations worth seeing Buses & taxis

The Moscow metro opened in 1935, designed as a "Palace of the People" — stations built with marble, mosaics, chandeliers, and epic bas-reliefs. Today it is the busiest metro system in Europe and one of the most efficient in the world. As a resident rather than a tourist, it will become the backbone of your daily life within the first week.

The network consists of 14 conventional metro lines, the Big Circle Line (Line 11 — the orbital ring around the city), the Moscow Central Circle (MCC — an overground ring), and the Moscow Central Diameters (MCD — suburban rail lines integrated with the Troika card system). The most recent line, Troitskaya (Line 16), opened in September 2024.

Signs throughout the system are in both Russian (Cyrillic) and English. You do not need to read Russian to navigate the Moscow metro, though learning the Cyrillic alphabet (achievable in a few days) makes everything significantly easier.

How to pay — critical for expats in 2026

The payment landscape changed fundamentally in March 2022 and has not reverted. This is the single most important thing to understand before your first metro journey.

✓ Works at the turnstile
  • Physical Troika card (loaded with credit)
  • Cash rubles (at ticket office or machine)
  • Russian MIR bank card (tap directly)
  • Edinyi unlimited day/period passes
✗ Does not work since 2022
  • Visa/Mastercard issued outside Russia
  • American Express, JCB (any non-Russian card)
  • Apple Pay / Google Pay with foreign cards
  • Samsung Pay with foreign cards
The practical answer for new arrivals: arrive with cash rubles, go to any metro ticket office, buy a Troika card (₽150 refundable deposit), load it with ₽1,000–2,000 of credit, and you're set for your first week. Machines have an English-language option.

The Troika card (Тройка) — your daily transport companion

The Troika is a reloadable plastic card that works across the entire Moscow public transport network — metro, MCC, MCD suburban rail (central zone), buses, trams, electric buses, electric river boats, and the monorail. As a resident, this is the only ticketing option you need.

Getting and loading a Troika

  • Where to buy: at any metro station ticket office, or at the yellow-and-green Troika vending machines in station lobbies. Look for the blue Troika sign.
  • Deposit: ₽150 (refunded when you return the card to any ticket office)
  • Top-up options: ticket office (cash), vending machine (cash), online at troika.mos.ru, or via the Moscow Metro app
  • Lost card: report at any large station ticket office; staff can transfer the balance to a new card
  • Usage: tap on entry at the turnstile. Do not tap on exit — only tap on entry.

Unlimited passes (Edinyi period passes)

For daily commuters, unlimited passes on the Troika card are far more economical. All cover metro, MCC, MCD Central zone, monorail, bus, e-bus, tram, and electric river boats. Passes start counting from first tap, not from purchase date. You have 10 days to activate them after purchase.

Essential apps for metro navigation

Yandex Metro

The essential app. Free, available in English with Latin script, offline map included, real-time route calculation including walking time between platforms at interchange stations. Download this before you arrive. Available globally on iOS and Android.

Download before arrival

Yandex Maps

Door-to-door navigation including metro legs, walking segments, bus connections, and Yandex taxi options. English-language interface. More useful than Google Maps for Moscow public transport routing.

Download before arrival

2GIS

Detailed offline maps of Moscow with businesses, addresses, and transport routes. Useful backup when you don't have data. Particularly good for finding addresses in Cyrillic.

Good offline backup

Troika app / troika.mos.ru

Official Troika top-up and balance check. The website works without a Russian account. The app requires a Russian phone number and is not on Western app stores — use the website instead for top-ups once you have a Russian bank card.

Website version recommended
The official "Метро Москвы" (Moscow Metro) app has been removed from Western App Store and Google Play due to sanctions. It can only be downloaded from RuStore (Russian Android store) and requires a Russian account for most features. As a resident, Yandex Metro is a better alternative anyway.

Stations worth a detour — the "Palace" stations

The Moscow metro was built as a statement — Stalin-era stations are genuine architectural achievements. As a resident, you'll pass through many of them daily; these are the ones worth pausing at.

Komsomolskaya (Circle Line)

The most spectacular station in the system — a Baroque palace underground, with enormous mosaics, chandeliers, and gilded vaulting. Exit above ground to the cluster of three major railway stations (Leningradsky, Yaroslavsky, Kazansky).

Mayakovskaya

Art Deco masterpiece with oval ceiling mosaics depicting Soviet aviation. Won the Grand Prix at the 1939 World's Fair in New York. One of the most photographed stations. Near Patriarshiye Prudy — likely to be on your daily route.

Novoslobodskaya (Circle Line)

32 stained-glass panels illuminated from within. Each panel depicts a different subject — one of the few stations in the world where the architecture itself tells stories through coloured light.

Park Pobedy (Line 3)

Deepest station in the system at 84 metres, with a 126-metre escalator descent that feels extraordinary. Two of the world's longest escalators. White marble mosaics depicting the 1812 and 1945 victories.

Kiyevskaya (Circle Line)

Large mosaics depicting scenes from Ukrainian history and Soviet-era Ukraine. Historically important and aesthetically striking. Adjacent to Kiyevsky railway station for trains south-west.

Elektrozavodskaya (Line 3)

Six rows of circular light fixtures create a remarkable tunnel effect. Industrial Art Deco celebrating factory workers, built during WWII. Compact but deeply atmospheric.

Beyond the metro — buses, taxis, and getting around Moscow

Buses and trams

Moscow's bus network covers virtually every major road, with modern (often electric) buses operating from approximately 5:30 to 1:00. Payment is via Troika card (tap on entry) — the same card used for the metro. Night buses (labelled "N" routes) operate along major avenues throughout the night. Route information is on the Moscow Transport app and at stops.

Yandex Go (taxis)

Yandex Go is the dominant ride-hailing app in Moscow — think of it as the local Uber. Works well, English-language app available, and very affordable compared to Western equivalents. A central-to-central Moscow journey typically costs ₽200–₽600 depending on time of day and traffic. Download and set up Yandex Go before arrival — it requires a Russian or foreign phone number for verification.

Important: Yandex Go prices surge significantly during peak hours and bad weather. The metro will nearly always beat a taxi for speed during rush hours.

Moscow Central Circle (MCC) and Central Diameters (MCD)

The MCC (Line 14 on the metro map) is an overground ring that circles the city, useful for cross-town journeys without going through the centre. The MCD lines are integrated suburban rail lines that use the same Troika card for the central zone. Both are shown on Yandex Metro and the official map.
