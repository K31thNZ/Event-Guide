Healthcare for English-speaking expats in Moscow

Moscow has excellent private medical facilities with English-speaking staff. Here's how to navigate the system, what insurance you need, and who to call in an emergency.

The healthcare landscape for expats

Moscow has a two-tier healthcare system. The Russian public system (polikliniki and state hospitals) is free for Russian citizens and available to registered foreign residents, but is Russian-language only and can involve long waits. Almost all English-speaking expats use the private sector for routine and specialist care.

The good news: Moscow's private medical sector is genuinely excellent. Several international-standard clinics employ doctors trained in the UK, US, Germany, and Israel. English-speaking GPs, specialists, and consultants are available, and facilities are modern. The cost is significantly lower than Western private healthcare — a GP consultation that might cost £250 in London typically runs €100–€150 in Moscow's top clinics.

Before you arrive: Sort international health insurance before leaving your home country. Policies purchased after you arrive in Russia are more expensive and may exclude pre-existing conditions. See the insurance section below.

English-speaking clinic directory

These are the clinics the expat community has historically used most. Opening hours, specific services, and availability change — we recommend calling ahead or visiting their websites for current information. For personalised recommendations, contact us.

European Medical Centre (EMC)

Full-service hospital

One of Moscow's most established international hospitals. Full range of services including A&E equivalent, maternity, paediatrics, and oncology. English-speaking staff throughout. Works with most international insurers. Multiple locations in Moscow.

Spiridonevsky lane 5, and other locations
emcmos.ru

Medsi International

Multi-clinic network

Large private clinic network with dedicated international patient departments. English-speaking coordinators available. Good for routine GP visits, specialist referrals, and diagnostics. Wide network across Moscow means one is usually nearby.

Multiple locations across Moscow
medsi.ru

CM Clinics (СМ-Клиника)

GP & specialist

Popular with long-term expat residents for routine care. More affordable than EMC while maintaining good standards. English-speaking staff at most central locations. Good for families needing regular paediatric care.

Multiple central Moscow locations
smclinic.ru

Invitro Diagnostics

Lab & diagnostics

Russia's largest private diagnostic laboratory chain. Blood tests, urinalysis, genetic testing, and imaging. Extremely convenient — collect point in almost every neighbourhood, results online. Very affordable. English-language website available.

100+ collection points in Moscow
invitro.ru
Tip on insurance and billing: Always confirm that your insurance is accepted before your appointment, not after. Some clinics operate as cash-pay only with reimbursement via your insurer — others bill the insurer directly. This distinction matters significantly at the end of a consultation.

Health insurance for expats

All expats living in Moscow should have international health insurance, or at minimum comprehensive travel insurance that covers medical evacuation. The Russian public health system is available to registered foreign residents but is not designed for English-speaking patients.

If your employer is sending you to Moscow, check whether they provide insurance — many corporate postings include international health cover. If you're moving independently, you'll need to arrange this yourself.

What to look for in a policy

  • Direct billing to Moscow's major private clinics — ideally EMC and Medsi at minimum
  • Medical evacuation cover — important given indirect flight routing; evacuation is complex and expensive
  • Valid in Russia — some policies exclude Russia; verify explicitly
  • Maternity cover if relevant — check waiting periods (typically 10–12 months before cover begins)
  • Mental health cover — increasingly important and increasingly available in international policies
Insurance for expats in Russia has become more complex since 2022. Some international insurers have withdrawn from the Russian market or restricted coverage. For current recommendations on insurers actively covering Moscow-based expats, contact us.

Pharmacies (аптека)

Pharmacies are extremely well distributed across Moscow — there is an аптека (apteka) in virtually every neighbourhood, often several within a few minutes' walk. They are typically open long hours; many are 24-hour. Major chains include Apteka 36.6, Rigla, and Samson-Pharma.

Many medications available only on prescription in Western countries are available over the counter in Russian pharmacies. Staff generally speak Russian only — having the generic (INN) drug name written down is more effective than a brand name, and the Cyrillic spelling helps enormously. Google Translate's camera mode works well in pharmacies.

Note: Some medications commonly used in Western countries are controlled substances in Russia and may not be available, or require specific prescriptions from a Russian doctor. This applies to some ADHD medications, certain anxiolytics, and some sleep aids. If you take regular medication, research its status in Russia before you arrive and bring a sufficient supply from home.

Dental & specialist care

Dental care in Moscow is of high quality and significantly cheaper than in Western countries. Private dental clinics are abundant; English-speaking dentists are available at the major private clinics listed above and at dedicated dental chains. A routine check-up and clean typically costs ₽3,000–₽6,000.

Specialist care — cardiology, orthopaedics, neurology, ophthalmology — is available at EMC and Medsi. For highly specialist procedures, some expats choose to travel to Europe rather than use Moscow facilities; with good insurance and indirect flights this remains practical.

Mental health support

English-language mental health support is available in Moscow, though the options are more limited than for physical healthcare. EMC has English-speaking psychologists and psychiatrists. Several private therapists offer English-language sessions — some in person, some online.

The expat experience — distance from family, language isolation, a significantly changed social environment — can be genuinely challenging. Many long-term expats find building community connections (see the Connect section) is as important as formal mental health support. Don't underestimate the value of the expat network.

If you are struggling and would like help finding English-language support in Moscow, contact us — we can connect you with resources and people who've navigated this.
