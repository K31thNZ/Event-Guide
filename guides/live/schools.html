International schools in Moscow

Moscow has over 20 international schools still operating in 2026, following curricula from the UK, US, IB and other systems. Here's what to know before you apply.

The international school landscape in 2026

Moscow has over 20 international schools catering to expatriate families, making it one of the better-served cities in Europe for international education. Most follow British, American, or International Baccalaureate (IB) curricula and teach primarily in English. They are open to both international and local Russian students.

The expat school population has contracted since 2022 as the Western community has shrunk, which has had mixed effects: some schools have reduced class sizes (which benefits remaining students) while others have consolidated campuses or reviewed their operations. All schools listed below were operating as of June 2026. We recommend confirming current status directly with the school before applying.

International School of Moscow (ISM)

Nord Anglia Education network · Part of 70+ global schools
British curriculum Ages 2–18

The most prominent British international school in Moscow, part of the Nord Anglia network. Follows the English National Curriculum from Early Years through A-Levels and Sixth Form. Collaborates with MIT and UNICEF through the Nord Anglia network. Strong track record of university placements in the UK, US, and Europe. ISM has been the school of choice for many diplomatic and corporate families since opening in 2007.

Curricula compared

British curriculum (GCSE / A-Level)

Follows the English National Curriculum through IGCSE (age 14–16) and A-Levels (16–18). Continuous assessment combined with final examinations. Strong preparation for UK university applications (UCAS) and internationally recognised. Emphasis on critical thinking and independent learning. ISM and BIS Moscow follow this system.

Best for: families planning to return to the UK or apply to UK/European universities

American curriculum (AP / High School Diploma)

Grade-based progression through High School Diploma. Advanced Placement (AP) courses prepare students for US college applications. Student participation, project-based learning, and extracurricular activities emphasised. Anglo-American School follows this system.

Best for: families planning US university applications or returning to the US

International Baccalaureate (IB)

Globally recognised curriculum for ages 3–19. IB Primary Years (PYP), Middle Years (MYP), and Diploma Programme (DP). Emphasises intercultural awareness and academic rigour. Recognised by universities worldwide and well-suited for families who move frequently. Several Moscow schools offer IB alongside their national curriculum.

Best for: frequently relocating families, global university applications

International Primary Curriculum (IPC)

Globally recognised curriculum for ages 5–12, based on British international schools. Prioritises intercultural awareness and student engagement. Each subject has learning goals tied to personal and community development. Used by several Moscow international schools at primary level.

Best for: primary-age children needing a flexible international framework

Fees and costs — what to budget

International school fees in Moscow are significant but remain lower than comparable schools in Western cities or expat hubs like Singapore or Dubai. Annual tuition at premium institutions ranges from ₽1,600,000 to ₽3,500,000 (approximately $17,500–$38,000 at 2026 rates), with mid-tier schools charging ₽800,000–₽1,600,000.

Saving on fees: ISM offers a 2% early-bird discount for full-year payments made before mid-June. Sibling discounts on enrolment fees range from 10% (second child) to 50% (fourth child). Some schools also offer family referral schemes. Ask the admissions team directly about available reductions.

The application process

Most schools accept applications year-round, but spaces are not always available — especially at primary level. The process typically involves:

  1. Initial enquiry and tour — contact the admissions team to arrange a school visit. This is strongly recommended before applying.
  2. Application and assessment — submit academic records from previous schools. Most schools conduct an assessment (interview and/or written tests) before offering a place.
  3. Offer and enrolment fee — if offered a place, you confirm by paying the enrolment fee. Places are not reserved without this.
  4. Tuition payment — annual or termly. Early-bird discounts for full-year payment at some schools.

For children transitioning from other international school systems, credit transfer is straightforward. Russian curriculum credit transfer to/from international schools is more complex — speak to the admissions team about your specific situation.

Location and commute — plan this carefully

School location should be a primary factor in your neighbourhood choice. Moscow traffic during school run hours (7:30–9:00 and 14:30–18:30) is severe. A commute that takes 25 minutes outside peak hours can take 90 minutes in morning traffic.

Most families either choose a neighbourhood close to their preferred school, or rely on the school's own bus service. The Anglo-American School and ISM are both in the north-west of the city, making that corridor particularly popular with expat families.
