Tax for foreign residents in Russia — 2026

The 183-day residency threshold, Russia's 2025 progressive tax reform, HQS advantages, and how your overseas income is treated.

Updated June 2026 10 min read
Jump to: 183-day rule2026 ratesHQS advantageRemote workersOverseas incomeFiling a return
Not tax advice. Russian tax law is complex and changes frequently. This guide explains the framework. For your personal situation — especially with overseas income, property, or investments — consult a qualified tax professional in both Russia and your home country before and after arriving.

Russia's tax system is straightforward in structure but has important nuances for foreign residents, particularly around the 183-day residency threshold, the 2025 progressive tax reform, and the treatment of overseas income. Understanding these before you arrive can prevent expensive surprises.

The 183-day rule — the most important number

Russia determines your tax status based purely on physical presence. If you spend 183 or more days in Russia in a calendar year, you become a tax resident. If you spend fewer, you are a non-resident for that year. Your status is assessed on 31 December each year — meaning it applies retroactively to the entire year based on where you ended up.

✓ Tax resident (183+ days)
  • Progressive rates 13–22% on Russian income
  • Worldwide income taxable in Russia
  • Can claim deductions and allowances
  • HQS: flat 13% from day one
✗ Non-resident (under 183 days)
  • Flat 30% on most Russian income
  • 15% on Russian dividends
  • No deductions available
  • Foreign income not taxable in Russia
The retroactive trap: If you work in Moscow from January to July, your employer withholds 13% tax assuming you'll become a resident. If you then leave Russia and don't return, ending the year under 183 days, you retroactively become a non-resident for the whole year. Your employer must then recalculate at 30% — and you owe the difference on months already passed. Plan your travel carefully.

2026 income tax rates for residents

Russia introduced a progressive tax scale in 2025, replacing the previous flat rates. The new bands effective from 2025 (first filed in 2026) are:

HQS tax advantage

Highly Qualified Specialists (HQS) have a significant tax advantage: they pay 13% personal income tax from their first day of employment in Russia, regardless of whether they have yet achieved 183 days of residence. Regular foreign workers pay 30% until they cross the 183-day threshold. This is one of the most financially significant benefits of the HQS status for senior professionals.

Remote workers and foreign employers (2024 onwards)

From 1 January 2024, a new rule applies to remote employees working for Russian companies or Russian branches of foreign companies: all such income is taxed at 13–15% regardless of the employee's tax residency status. The previous 30% rate for non-residents no longer applies to this specific category. However, this rule applies only to employment contracts — civil law contracts (freelance/consulting) remain subject to the standard 30% non-resident rate.

From 2025, a further extension applies: individuals receiving income from Russian companies for services performed using Russian domain names or network addresses also fall under the 13% rate even if working abroad.

Overseas income and worldwide taxation

Once you become a Russian tax resident (183+ days), Russia taxes your worldwide income — including salary from foreign employers, rental income from property abroad, dividends and interest from foreign accounts, and capital gains from overseas investments. You must declare this income on your annual Russian tax return.

Russia has double taxation treaties with over 80 countries. For most Western expats, these treaties prevent being taxed twice on the same income. However, since 2022, Russia has been in discussions about modifying or suspending treaty provisions with countries it designates as "unfriendly" — primarily EU countries, the UK, US, Canada, and Australia. The current status of specific treaties should be verified with a qualified tax professional.

Getting your INN (tax number)

All foreign residents in Russia working or receiving Russian income need an INN (ИНН — Индивидуальный Номер Налогоплательщика), a 12-digit personal tax identification number. Your employer typically assists with obtaining one. Alternatively, you can apply at any FTS (Federal Tax Service) office with your passport and migration registration document. The INN is also required to open a Russian bank account.

Filing a tax return

If your employer is Russian and they correctly withheld tax on your salary, you typically do not need to file a separate personal tax return — your employer acts as a tax agent. You do need to file a return (Form 3-NDFL) if you have: income from foreign sources, income from selling property, rental income from property you own, or multiple employers who may not have coordinated your total income correctly. The deadline for the previous year's return is 30 April of the following year; payment of any additional tax due is by 15 July.

Tax situation questions?

Cross-border tax — especially with overseas income or property — is complex. We can refer you to English-speaking tax advisers familiar with the Russia expat situation.
